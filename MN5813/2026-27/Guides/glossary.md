## Module vocabulary

- <a id="term-abcd-record"></a>**abcd record (the record)**: The dataset for the individual report. It is the written record that abcd, a small open-source software tool, keeps of its own work, as it stood at one release. That work is its issues, intents, specs, decisions, principles, commits, and releases. `Tools/fetch_abcd.py` fetches it once into ten tables. The project is the module leader's own, treated as a client. Findings that criticise it are expected. Introduced in abcd-record.md, section "Part 1: The record, in plain words", and the client in section "The client"
- <a id="term-ai-failure-gallery"></a>**AI failure gallery**: The closing section of every Debrief notebook, headed `## 🤖 AI failure gallery`. It shows two to four realistic ways an AI tool gets that week's material wrong. For each, it says why it is wrong in business terms and what would have shown you the error. Introduced in [ai-guide.md](./ai-guide.md), section "5. Verification". The first gallery is in [Week01/Debrief.ipynb](../Week/01/Debrief.ipynb), section "🤖 AI failure gallery"
- <a id="term-ai-lens"></a>**AI Lens**: A short callout, in a cell of its own. It asks you to try one exchange with your Associate about the cell in front of you. It is a blockquote beginning `> 🤖 **AI Lens:**` saying what to do and why. The prompt follows in a fenced block to paste as it stands. Then a line says what a good answer contains. Every AI Lens prompt is also collected in [prompts.md](./prompts.md). The first one is in [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "3. f-strings: Readable output"
- <a id="term-ai-use-record"></a>**AI Use Record**: The declaration and summary of AI involvement that every assessed submission includes, whichever tools you used. It lists the tools, what each was used for, notable rejections and corrections, and a short reflection. Its specification is in interpretation.md, section "The AI Use Record: The canonical specification". The working habit behind it is in [ai-guide.md](./ai-guide.md), section "7. The AI Use Record"
- <a id="term-analyst"></a>**Analyst**: You, in the module's fiction. You are the Junior Analyst at Runnymede Analytics: You direct the Associate, carry the judgement, and sign the work. Introduced in the opening memo of [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "📋 A memo from the Partner"
- <a id="term-assessment"></a>**Assessment**: A week that prepares you for the module's two assessments. The group project gets feedback and counts for no marks. The individual report is your module mark. Introduced in Assessment/README.md
- <a id="term-associate"></a>**Associate**: The module's name for your AI tool, whichever one you use. It is GitHub Copilot in your Codespace, the agent in your Stoa room, or Gemini in Colab during Week 1. Introduced in [ai-guide.md](./ai-guide.md), section "2. Meet your Associate", and in [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Meet your Associate"
- <a id="term-badge"></a>**badge**: The marker of the tier you completed in a quest (Bronze, Silver, or Gold), claimed by you, spot-checked, and recorded in your QuestLog. It carries no marks. Introduced in [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Quests and badges"
- <a id="term-brief"></a>**brief**: One of the seven starting points for the individual report. Each names a stakeholder, a starting question, a required primary table with its slice and measure, a chart family, and a risk. You choose and declare one in Week 6, first come first served, at most two students per brief on different slices. You sharpen its question into the one your report answers. Introduced in individual-briefs.md. The report brief is individual-report.md, section "1. The brief"
- <a id="term-bronze"></a>**Bronze**: The first tier of a quest's Build stage, expected of every student. You reach it by prompting the Associate well and checking its output against the Definition of Done. The first Bronze is in [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze" <!-- values-guard: allow: a quest stage estimate, not the lecture length -->
- <a id="term-bug-hunt"></a>**BUG-HUNT**: The third AI move and the move of the Red Flags stage. The Associate is the suspect and you are the auditor: You find the working-but-wrong code, explain the harm in business terms, and direct the fix. Introduced in [ai-guide.md](./ai-guide.md), section "BUG-HUNT: The Associate is the suspect; you are the auditor", and [Week02/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02/Introduction.ipynb), section "The four \"moves\", formally"
- <a id="term-commit"></a>**commit**: One saved change to the abcd project's files. Each has a timestamp, an author, a one-line message, and the files it touched. The message's first word says the kind of change (`feat`, `fix`, `docs`, and so on). The record keeps them in the `commits` and `commit_files` tables. Introduced in abcd-record.md, section "Part 1: The record, in plain words"
- <a id="term-crit-entry"></a>**`CRIT` entry**: The Week 9 convention for typing a Four-D critique into your Stoa room. It is one scorecard line prefixed `CRIT`, one per figure per round. Introduced in [stoa-workflow.md](./stoa-workflow.md), section "A second prefix from Week 9: `CRIT`", and Week09/Introduction.ipynb, section "The scorecard"
- <a id="term-debrief"></a>**Debrief**: The term names two related things. The Debrief stage (Stage 5) closes every quest with three fixed TEACH-BACK prompts: A client explanation, an Associate audit, and a quiz. `Debrief.ipynb` is the week's notebook of worked answers, read after the quest, ending with the AI failure gallery. Introduced in [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "The weekly rhythm"
- <a id="term-decision"></a>**decision (ADR)**: An architecture decision record in the abcd record. It is a dated, written ruling on how something is done and why, sometimes superseded by a later one. The record keeps them in the `decisions` table. Use its `date` field, not `first_commit`, for when a decision was made. Introduced in abcd-record.md, section "Part 1: The record, in plain words"
- <a id="term-decision-entry"></a>**`DECISION:` entry**: The convention for logging a significant team agreement in your Stoa room. It is the prefix `DECISION:`, then what you decided, why, and who owns the resulting action. Introduced in [stoa-workflow.md](./stoa-workflow.md), section "4. The DECISION: convention". Practised in Week07/Introduction.ipynb, section "0:45–1:15: Cleaning decisions"
- <a id="term-definition-of-done"></a>**Definition of Done**: The short self-check list in every quest Briefing that says what "complete" means for that job. Check your work against it before you ship. The first one is in [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "📋 Stage 1: Briefing"
- <a id="term-demonstration"></a>**Demonstration**: The week's notebook of worked examples, `Demonstration.ipynb`. It shows each of the week's techniques in code you can run. You work through it before the quest. Introduced in the module [README.md](../README.md), section "Recommended way forward". The first is [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb)
- <a id="term-design-rationale"></a>**design rationale**: The written explanation, usually 150-300 words, that goes with every key figure in both assessments. It gives the question, why this chart form, the alternatives rejected, and what changed between the Associate's draft and your figure. Introduced in dataviz-design-guide.md, section "6. E: Explain the design rationale". The template is in Assessment/Template.ipynb, section "Design rationale: Figure 1: [figure title]"
- <a id="term-exercises"></a>**Exercises**: The week's practice notebook for the workshop, `Exercises.ipynb`, in a week that has one. You work through it in the workshop, with staff there to help. Introduced in the module [README.md](../README.md), section "Recommended way forward". The first is [Week02/Exercises.ipynb](../Week/02/Exercises.ipynb)
- <a id="term-first-look"></a>**First look**: Stage 2 of every quest. It has three or four cells "your Associate drafted overnight". Each is preceded by a prediction cell you fill in before running. PREDICT-RUN lives here. It first appears in [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🔍 Stage 2: First look"
- <a id="term-four-d-crit"></a>**Four-D crit**: The peer-critique protocol of Week 9's Gallery, in four rounds. In order, they are Describe, Decode, Diagnose (score against TRACE), and Redesign (each critic proposes exactly one change). Authors stay silent until the last round. Introduced in dataviz-design-guide.md, section "8. The Four-D crit protocol", and Week09/Introduction.ipynb, section "The Four-D protocol, spelled out"
- <a id="term-gallery"></a>**Gallery**: Week 9's session: A peer critique of group-project figures using the Four-D protocol. Each figure gets a TRACE scorecard, and outcomes are logged as `CRIT` and `DECISION:` entries. Introduced in Week09/Introduction.ipynb, section "No quest this week: The crit is the work". Modelled in full in Week09/Demonstration.ipynb, section "The crit, transcribed"
- <a id="term-generate-judge"></a>**GENERATE-JUDGE**: The first AI move and the move of the Build stage: The Associate drafts, and you accept or reject with reasons you can state. Introduced in [ai-guide.md](./ai-guide.md), section "GENERATE-JUDGE: The Associate drafts; you accept or reject, with reasons", and [Week02/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02/Introduction.ipynb), section "The four \"moves\", formally"
- <a id="term-gold"></a>**Gold**: The third tier of a quest's Build stage. It is an open-ended stretch with no ceiling, such as a harder business question, an interactive figure, or an extension you design yourself. The first Gold is in [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥇 Gold" <!-- values-guard: allow: a quest stage estimate, not the lecture length -->
- <a id="term-intent"></a>**intent**: A record of a promise in the abcd record. It is written as the press release the project would publish if the promise were kept. It moves from draft to planned to shipped or superseded. The record keeps them in the `intents` table. Introduced in abcd-record.md, section "Part 1: The record, in plain words"
- <a id="term-issue"></a>**issue**: A record of a problem someone noticed in the abcd project. It has a severity, a category, a note of how it was found, and a status. The status is open, resolved with a note of what fixed it, or closed with a reason for not fixing it. The record keeps them in the `issues` table. Most briefs merge other tables with it. Introduced in abcd-record.md, section "Part 1: The record, in plain words"
- <a id="term-predict-run"></a>**PREDICT-RUN**: The second AI move and the move of the First look stage. You write down what a cell will output before you run it, then the machine reveals the answer. Every surprise marks a gap in your understanding. Introduced in [ai-guide.md](./ai-guide.md), section "PREDICT-RUN: You predict; the machine reveals", and [Week02/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02/Introduction.ipynb), section "The four \"moves\", formally"

The cell itself is in [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "📔 Prompt Diary: Today's highlights"

It has Bronze, Silver, and Gold tiers and takes about ninety minutes. Introduced in [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Quests and badges". The first quest is [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb)

Introduced in [QuestLog-Template.md](../Week/01/QuestLog-Template.md), section "Why this file exists", and [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "The record you will keep"

You find it, explain it in business terms, and fix it, ideally by directing the Associate. BUG-HUNT lives here. The first is in [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🚩 Stage 4: Red Flags"
- <a id="term-release"></a>**release**: A version tag on one commit of the abcd project, the moment a set of changes was published under a number. The record keeps them in the `releases` table, 21 rows. Introduced in abcd-record.md, section "Part 1: The record, in plain words"
- <a id="term-runnymede-analytics"></a>**Runnymede Analytics**: The fictional boutique consultancy that frames the module. You join it as a Junior Analyst, the Partner sends the briefs, and every quest is a job for one of its clients. ### Python foundations

- **Variable**: A named label for a value, so you can use the value later by name. The `=` sign does the assigning. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "2. Variables: Labelled boxes". Used again: [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze", and [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Data types, and why you check them"
- **Data types (`int`, `float`, `str`)**: The kinds of value Python tells apart: Whole numbers, decimals, and text. Check a value's type before you trust arithmetic on it. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "1. Numbers and text". Used again: [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Data types, and why you check them", and [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥈 Silver" (a text price that breaks `sum()`)
- **f-string**: A string beginning `f"..."` in which `{...}` slots are filled with values. A format spec such as `:.2f` controls the decimals. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "3. f-strings: Readable output". Used again: [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze" (printing money as `£x.xx`). Failure gallery: Week02/Debrief.ipynb, entry "Failure 3: The f-string that silently rounds"
- **`if` / `else` and `elif`**: A two-way decision with `if`/`else`, and a multi-way decision with an `elif` chain in which the first true condition wins. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "4. Comparisons and decisions: `if` / `else`". `elif` is taught in [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Multi-way decisions with `elif`". Used again: [Week02/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02/Quest.ipynb), section "🥈 Silver" (the time-of-day slots)
- **List**: An ordered collection of values in square brackets, looked up by position. It is the first structure you use for many values under one name. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "5. Lists: Many values, one name". Used again: [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Lists: New techniques" (indexing, slicing, `sorted()` versus `.sort()`), and [Week02/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze"
- **`for` loop**: A loop that visits each item in a collection in turn and runs the indented block for it. The running total is its classic analyst use. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "6. for loops: Do something with every item". Used again: [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze", and [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "More loop patterns" (`range()`). Failure gallery: Week02/Debrief.ipynb, entry "Failure 2: The off-by-one `range()` loop"
- **`while` loop**: A loop with no fixed count that repeats for as long as a condition stays true. A condition that never turns false runs forever. First taught: [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "More loop patterns". Used again: [Week02b/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02b/Demonstration.ipynb) (optional Week 2b), section "A first class: Data and behaviour in one box" (redeeming loyalty stamps ten at a time)
- **Built-in functions (`len`, `sum`, `min`, `max`)**: Python's ready-made functions for counting and totalling a collection. `sum(...) / len(...)` gives an average. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "7. len, sum, min, max: The shortcuts". Used again: [Week01/Quest.ipynb](../Week/01/Quest.ipynb), section "🥈 Silver"
- **Function**: A named, reusable block of code defined with `def`. Inputs go in as parameters, a result comes out through `return`, and a docstring (a note inside the function) says what it is for. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "8. Functions: Calling vs defining". Parameters, defaults, and `return` are taught in [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Functions: Code you can reuse". Used again: [Week02/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02/Quest.ipynb), section "🥈 Silver" (`slot_for()`). Failure gallery: Week02b/Debrief.ipynb (optional Week 2b), entry "Failure 2: The mutable default argument, reports that haunt each other"
- **Reading an error message**: The habit of reading a Python traceback (the error report) from the highlighted line to the last line. The last line names the error and says what went wrong. First taught: [Week01/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/01/Demonstration.ipynb), section "9. Reading an error message, top to bottom". Used again: [Week02/Exercises.ipynb](../Week/02/Exercises.ipynb), section "2. Cells run in the order YOU run them" (a `NameError`), and [Week02b/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02b/Demonstration.ipynb) (optional Week 2b), section "Error handling: Fail loudly, on purpose"
- **String methods**: Built-in operations on text, called with a dot (`.lower()`, `.strip()`, `.split()`, `.replace()`). Each returns a new string, so they can be chained. First taught: [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "String methods". Used again: [Week02/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02/Quest.ipynb), section "🥈 Silver" (`.split(":")` on a time), and Week07/Demonstration.ipynb, section "Part 2: Decisions, then cleaning". Failure gallery: Week02/Debrief.ipynb, entry "Failure 1: The hallucinated string method"
- **Dictionary**: A collection that stores values by name rather than by position, as key-value pairs in curly braces. `.get()` and `.items()` give safe lookup and looping. Much real business data arrives as a list of dictionaries. First taught: [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Dictionaries: Labelled data". Used again: [Week02/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze" (the counting pattern), and [Week02b/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02b/Introduction.ipynb) (optional Week 2b), section "Strategy 1: Trace the data flow"
- **File round-trip**: Writing a result to a file with `with open(..., "w")`, then reading it back with `with open(..., "r")` to prove it saved. First taught: [Week02/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02/Demonstration.ipynb), section "File handling: Write it, read it back" (first met in [Week02/Exercises.ipynb](../Week/02/Exercises.ipynb), section "6. Where files live"). Used again: [Week02/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02/Quest.ipynb), section "🥉 Bronze" (the founder's summary file), and, with pandas, Week04/Demonstration.ipynb, section "Reading and writing files"
- **Comprehension**: A list (or dictionary) built in one line from a loop and an optional filter: `[expression for item in items if condition]`. First taught: [Week02b/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02b/Introduction.ipynb) (optional Week 2b), section "1. Comprehensions: A list built in one move". Worked example in [Week02b/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02b/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Comprehensions: A list in one move". Used again: Week02b/Debrief.ipynb, section "🥉 Bronze: Churn per plan, worked". Failure gallery: Week02b/Debrief.ipynb, entry "Failure 3: The comprehension that silently drops records"
- **Lambda**: A small unnamed function written in the place it is used. It is most often used as the `key=` of `sorted()` to rank a collection by any field you choose. First taught: [Week02b/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02b/Introduction.ipynb) (optional Week 2b), section "2. `lambda` + `sorted(key=...)`: Rank anything by anything". Worked example in [Week02b/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02b/Demonstration.ipynb), section "`lambda` and `sorted(key=...)`: Rank anything by anything". Used again: Week04/Demonstration.ipynb, section "Applying functions to columns"
- **Exception (`try` / `except`)**: Catching an error you expect by naming that exception, so the program still fails loudly on the errors you did not expect. A bare `except:` hides every error. First taught: [Week02b/Introduction.ipynb](../Week/02b/Introduction.ipynb) (optional Week 2b), section "3. `try`/`except`: Failing on purpose, loudly". Worked example in [Week02b/Demonstration.ipynb](../Week/02b/Demonstration.ipynb), section "Error handling: Fail loudly, on purpose". Used again: [Week02b/Quest.ipynb](../Week/02b/Quest.ipynb), section "🚩 Stage 4: Red Flags". Failure gallery: Week02b/Debrief.ipynb, entry "Failure 1: The bare `except`, errors as landfill"
